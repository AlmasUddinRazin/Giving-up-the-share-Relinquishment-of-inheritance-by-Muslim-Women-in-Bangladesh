Islamic Sharia · Giving It Up

She may part with her share only by a real transaction

Gift (hiba)

After the death the share is hers, and she may give it away freely.

Sale

She may sell her share for a price.

Takharuj

All heirs agree she withdraws for property or money. Hanafi law treats it as an exchange, so it needs real value.

“…except trade by mutual consent.” Quran 4:29

Before deathShe has no right yet, only a hope, so a promise to give up her share transfers nothing.

Case Law · After Death and Under Pressure

Pakistan: relinquishment is against Islamic public policy

Pakistan · PLD 1990 SC 1

Ghulam Ali v. Ghulam Sarwar Naqvi

  • A sister was denied her share by her three brothers
  • The estate passes to heirs automatically and immediately
  • No brother can stand as an intermediary
  • A woman’s relinquishment is against public policy as understood in Islam
India · AIR 1963 SC 1203

Kharbuja Kuer v. Jangbahadur Rai

  • Deed signed by a pardanashin woman
  • Whoever relies on it must prove she understood it
  • It must be “not only her physical act but also her mental act”

Analysis

The law protects her at two stages, not at the third

StageShariaStatuteIs she protected?
1. Promise before deathNo right exists; nothing passesTPA s. 6(a): no transferYes, unless estoppel is applied
2. “I don’t want my share” after deathInheritance is compulsoryRegistration Act ss. 17, 49Yes: no legal effect
3. Registered release, sale or hebaValid only with free consent and real valuePresumed valid; she must sue, usually within 3 yearsWeakly: the burden is on her
The GapSharia asks whether she truly consented. The statute asks whether the deed was registered.

Our Recommendations

Test her consent before her share passes

1

Safeguards at registration

The sub-registrar explains the deed privately and records the value she receives

2

A legal presumption

Undue influence is presumed when she transfers to a male co-heir far below market value

3

No estoppel for token payments

Courts follow the reasoning in Ghulam Ali

4

Every heir on the record

Mutation lists each heir and her share in her own name

5

Legal aid and awareness

Women learn their share cannot be lost by silence or a promise

Conclusion
She cannot lose her share by a promise or a statement, but she can lose it by a registered deed signed under pressure.

Sharia already demands free consent and real value. Our law should demand the same before a woman’s share passes to her brother.
